## Patterns to borrow (what consistently shows up on converting pages)
1) Strong quantified outcome early, usually review volume growth or ranking impact.
2) Very clear automation trigger story, "after appointment" or "after job".
3) Simple, visual 3-step flow.
4) Proof density, either G2 badges, customer logos, named testimonials with outcomes.
5) Friction reducers, free trial, no credit card, cancel anytime.
6) Objection handling, "no spam", "no duplicate requests", "compliant".
7) Multi-channel asks, SMS plus email plus QR, often widgets and social proof reuse.
8) "Private feedback first" flow is common, often described as surveys, ticketing, routing.

# Two alternative landing page concepts for Surfaxe (meaningfully different from the current page)

Context: Current Surfaxe landing is a standard SaaS product page with hero, how-it-works, simple pricing ($49/mo plus 3 cents per text), and limited proof. It is positioned for pool service companies using Skimmer.

Below are two distinct angles and section structures, with draft copy and implementation notes.

## Concept A, "30-Day Review Sprint" performance offer (outcome-first, timed challenge)

### Why this is meaningfully different
- Shifts from "tool" to "program" with a defined time horizon and a specific target.
- Adds a strong conversion mechanism, a review goal calculator and a 30-day plan, similar to Reviewly and ReputationStacker time-to-value framing.
- Enables a higher-intent CTA, "Get my 30-day plan", capturing leads even if they are not ready to subscribe.

### Elements to borrow
- Time-to-value and setup speed language (Reviewly, ReputationStacker).
- Interactive demo and calculator style (GoSite, DemandHub tools).
- Outcome proof blocks with targets and benchmarks (NiceJob, Birdeye).

### Above the fold draft
- Tag: Built for pool service companies on Skimmer.
- H1: "Add 15 to 40 new Google reviews in the next 30 days, on autopilot".
- Subhead: "Surfaxe triggers a single, compliant text after each completed Skimmer visit, routes unhappy customers to private feedback, and tracks who already reviewed so you never spam your route.".
- Microproof row (icons): "No contracts", "No duplicate texts", "Live in 1 to 2 business days".
- Primary CTA: "Build my 30-day plan" (scrolls to calculator).
- Secondary CTA: "See sample text" (opens modal).

### Key sections
1) Calculator, "How many reviews can you get?"
   - Inputs: jobs per week, percent repeat customers, current review count.
   - Outputs: estimated invites, estimated new reviews (range), expected star rating lift.
   - CTA: "Email me this plan" with name, email, phone.
2) The 3-step sprint
   - Step 1: Connect Skimmer and your Google Business Profile.
   - Step 2: Auto-send one text 24 hours after service.
   - Step 3: Review routing, 4 to 5 goes to Google, 1 to 3 goes to private fix-it form.
3) Proof section, "What pool owners say" or "Early results"
   - If no testimonials, use anonymized mini case studies, "Company A, 9 reviews to 31 in 6 weeks".
4) "Compliance and customer-friendly" section
   - Emphasize no review gating, no incentives, opt-out language.
5) Pricing as an "all-in" estimate
   - Reframe pricing in outcome terms, example math by route size.
6) "Start the sprint" onboarding
   - 3 fields only, then schedule kickoff.
7) FAQ

## Concept B, "Protect your route" negative feedback capture, retention-first (risk and ops angle)

### Why this is meaningfully different
- Leads with preventing churn and saving accounts, not just getting 5-star reviews.
- Repositions Surfaxe as a customer experience safety net for recurring service routes.
- Uses "inbox, alerts, and fix-it tickets" language that mirrors Reputation.com and ServiceTitan.

### Elements to borrow
- "Actions and tickets" narrative (Reputation.com).
- Review to technician or job attribution story, adapted to route, tech, and account (ServiceTitan).
- 4.0+ star rating threshold framing (DemandHub).

### Above the fold draft
- Tag: Built for Skimmer pool service companies.
- H1: "Catch unhappy customers before they leave a 1-star review".
- Subhead: "Surfaxe automatically asks for feedback after service, sends happy customers to Google, and quietly alerts you when something went wrong so you can save the account.".
- Proof chips: "Private feedback alerts", "One text only", "Track who already reviewed".
- Primary CTA: "Turn on Feedback Alerts".
- Secondary CTA: "See how routing works".

### Key sections
1) Problem framing, "The silent churn"
   - Pool service is recurring, one bad visit can lose a $150 to $300 per month account.
2) "Feedback routing" diagram
   - Stars 1 to 3, goes to private form, alert sent.
   - Stars 4 to 5, goes to Google link.
3) Alerts and follow-up workflow
   - Simple queue: customer name, address, issue category, last service date.
   - Optional: assign to tech or office.
4) "Review growth is the side effect" section
   - Show how consistent 4 to 5 star asks lead to more reviews.
5) Pricing as insurance
   - "One saved account pays for a year" style math.
6) Implementation, "We do it for you"
   - Clarify setup takes 1 to 2 days, no tech training.
7) Trust section
   - "Compliant", "no incentives", "opt-out", "TCPA-friendly language".

### Draft copy blocks
- "You cannot control who has a bad day, you can control whether you hear about it first.".
- "One simple text, 24 hours after service, always at a sensible hour.".
- "Never ask the same customer twice, Surfaxe tracks reviewers automatically.".
